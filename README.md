# DSA-PROJECT-3
THIS IS WHERE I BUILT MY FIRST PORTFOLIO WHILE LEARNING AT THE INCUBATOR HUB
Students' Industrial Work Experience Scheme (SIWES): Summary Report
Fan Milk Plc, Eleyele Industrial Layout, Ibadan · Akindosu Oluwatosin Oluwadunsin (2022001681) · B.Tech Chemistry, LAUTECH
1. Introduction
SIWES is a compulsory six-month programme for university students, started by the Industrial Training Fund (ITF) in 1973. In the early 1970s, graduates were strong in theory but lacked the practical experience industries needed. SIWES was created to close that gap by placing students in real workplaces where they apply what they learned in class.
My training took place at Fan Milk Plc, a dairy and beverage company that makes SuperYogo (yogurt), Fanice and Go Slo (ice cream), FanChoco (chocolate milk drink) and Fandango (a non-dairy citrus drink).
•	Main posting: Production department (mixing, processing, packaging, ice cream)
•	Additional exposure: laboratory, water treatment plant and effluent treatment plant
2. Production Section
The production section, also called the factory, is where raw materials such as sugar, skimmed milk powder, whey, cocoa, stabilizers, flavors and colors are converted into finished products.
Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP)
GMP is a set of rules from regulatory authorities that food manufacturers must follow. Staff keep overalls and shoes clean, remove jewelry, carry no personal items, wash hands before entry, wear PPE and work only according to approved Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs).
Why it matters: food is easily contaminated, so these rules protect the consumer and keep every batch safe and consistent.
Mixing area
This is the first stage on the production line, where ingredients are measured out and combined.
•	Downloading: the required ingredient list and quantities for a product are generated by entering the product and volume into the control-room computer
•	Weighing: done manually on kilogram and gram scales. One wrongly weighed ingredient can spoil product quality or make a whole mix unsafe, so it must be done carefully
•	Debagging: powders such as skimmed milk powder, whey and cocoa are emptied into a funnel and sucked into the Almix, the pre-mixing tank
•	Mixing: final blending happens in tanks fitted with motor-driven agitators. Dairy and water-based products use separate sets of tanks
•	Pipe connections: must be correct, since a wrong connection could send a mix to the wrong tank or contaminate it with another product
Cleaning-in-Place (CIP): tanks and pipes are cleaned without dismantling them, using a warm water pre-rinse, a caustic soda (NaOH) wash and a hot water rinse. Caustic soda is a strong alkali that dissolves fat and protein residues left by milk products.
Processing area
The processing area turns the raw mix into a safe, consumable product. Each product follows its own path, so not every product passes through every step.
Filtration → Pasteurization → Homogenization (dairy only) → Fermentation (yogurt only) → Ageing (ice cream only)
•	Filtration: the mix is pulled through a filter in the pipeline to remove any foreign solid that may have fallen into the tank. All Fan Milk products go through this step
•	Pasteurization: a heat treatment at a set temperature and time that destroys harmful microorganisms and extends shelf life. The pasteurizer has four sections: preheating, heating, precooling and cooling. Dairy products use the APV or Lacta pasteurizers (high and lower capacity), while water-based products use the aseptic pasteurizer
•	Homogenization: the mix is forced through a tiny opening under very high pressure, in two stages, which breaks fat globules into very small particles. This gives a smooth emulsion so the cream does not separate and rise over time
•	Water-based products such as Fandango skip homogenization because they contain no milk fat
Yogurt (SuperYogo) production
Yogurt is a fermented product, so it needs microorganisms to convert milk sugar into acid.
•	Starter culture preparation: a bulk culture of Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus is kept dormant at -18°C or below. A solution of skimmed milk powder and water is filtered, pasteurized and homogenized, then the culture is added in the starter tank. It takes at least 8 hours, until the pH reaches 4.2–4.7, so it is prepared hours before production
•	Inoculation: the prepared starter is added to the processed mix at about 3.3% (ratio 1:29). For example, 12,000 L of yogurt needs 400 L of starter and 11,600 L of mix
•	Fermentation: the mix is agitated for about 10 minutes, then held at 40–44°C for at least 4 hours in the Fermentation Room, a high-care enclosed area
Lactose → glucose + galactose → pyruvate → lactic acid → lower pH → casein coagulation → yogurt texture
In simple terms: the bacteria feed on lactose and release lactic acid. The acid lowers the pH, which makes the milk protein (casein) clump together, giving yogurt its sour taste and thick texture.
Ice cream section
Ice cream is a sweetened frozen food made from milk or cream, flavors, colors and stabilizers. The mix is stirred to trap air and cooled below freezing so that no large ice crystals form.
•	Ageing: the mix rests at -4°C or below for at least 4 hours (ideally overnight). This lets milk proteins and stabilizers fully hydrate, which improves smoothness, whipping ability (overrun), fat crystallization, melt resistance and flavor blending
•	Freezing: the Frosto machine adds ice crystals and air. Smaller crystals give a smooth, creamy texture, while larger ones make it rough and icy
•	Packing: scoopable ice cream is packed in cups (120 ml, 250 ml) and tubs (450 ml to 3 L). Tubs are coded, then washed with a 1% chlorine solution before use
Packaging
This is where processed products are packed into pouches and prepared for cold storage and distribution. It takes up about half of the production floor.
•	Quality check: a QA officer examines color, taste, viscosity, pH and homogeneity. A batch that meets the specification is released, while one that fails is held until the laboratory decides whether to modify or destroy it
•	In-feed: pouch filling machines (DJ lines and Thimonnier) fill and seal sachets and print the production date, expiry date and mix number. Thimonnier is the most automated and efficient
•	Scanico freezer: sachets are frozen at -18°C to -23°C using ammonia refrigeration. Products travel in a spiral path, so each takes about 35–45 minutes to pass through
•	Out-feed: frozen sachets are counted into nylon wraps (for example 25 pieces for 90 ml packs and 15 for 125 ml), sealed, stacked and moved to cold rooms
3. Laboratory: Water Analysis
Water analysis checks that the water meets the quality needed for production. Parameters tested include pH, conductivity, total dissolved solids (TDS), chloride, hardness and alkalinity.
•	Conductivity: measured with a meter, and shows how well water conducts electricity, which depends on dissolved ions such as Na⁺, Ca²⁺, Cl⁻ and SO₄²⁻. It is reported in µS/cm
•	Alkalinity: determined by titration with a standard acid, reported as mg/L CaCO₃. It measures the water's ability to neutralize acid, mainly from hydroxide, carbonate and bicarbonate ions
Why it matters: water can look clear and still contain dissolved salts, microorganisms or residual chlorine. Measurements let the team monitor and control the treatment process.
4. Water Treatment Plant
Raw water comes from Eleyele Dam by underground pipeline. It can contain suspended solids, clay, organic matter, microorganisms and color, and its quality changes with rainfall and season, so it cannot go straight into production.
Raw water tank → strainer → sedimentation → alum coagulation → lamella clarifier → chlorination → sand filter → underground tank → activated carbon → micron filters → UV → small tank → micron filters → final sterilization → utility
Stage	What it does and why
Strainer	Removes coarse debris such as leaves and rubber, protecting pumps, valves and filters
Sedimentation	Heavier particles settle to the bottom under gravity
Aluminium sulfate (alum)	Releases Al³⁺, which forms Al(OH)₃ flocs. Tiny particles carry electrical charges that keep them apart, and alum neutralizes this so they clump into larger flocs. The reaction also releases H⁺, which lowers pH, so enough alkalinity is needed
Lamella clarifier	Inclined plates give a large settling area in a small space, so flocs settle out and the water becomes clear
Chlorine	Reacts with water to form hypochlorous acid (HOCl), which enters microbial cells and inactivates them. It works best at lower pH, where more chlorine exists as HOCl
Sand filter	Traps remaining suspended solids and fine flocs
Activated carbon	Its porous surface adsorbs residual chlorine, color, taste and odor compounds
Micron filters	Retain fine particles according to pore size; smaller pores retain smaller particles
UV sterilization	UV-C light damages microbial DNA so they cannot replicate. It adds no chemicals but leaves no residual protection
Repeated filtration and sterilization give several barriers against contamination, an approach known as progressive purification. Treated water also protects equipment from fouling and scaling.
5. Effluent (Wastewater) Treatment Plant
Wastewater from all factory departments contains suspended solids, fats, oils, product residues and cleaning chemicals. It must be treated before release to protect the environment.
Primary treatment
The first stage removes large, floating and settleable material before chemicals are added.
Receiving pit → sedimentation pit → fat and oil removal → straining → discharge pit → storage tank → manhole → Control Central Unit → buffer tank
•	Receiving pit: collects wastewater streams from different departments
•	Sedimentation pit: heavy solids settle; a level switch and submersible pump manage the flow
•	Fat and oil removal: fats are less dense than water, so they rise to the surface and are separated
•	Storage and buffer tanks: even out the flow, since wastewater generation varies through the day and chemical dosing needs steady flow
Secondary chemical treatment
•	Ferric chloride (FeCl₃): the coagulant. Fe³⁺ hydrolyzes to form Fe(OH)₃ flocs that trap fine particles, and it also releases H⁺, which lowers pH
•	Caustic soda (NaOH): supplies OH⁻, which neutralizes the H⁺ and restores the pH to the desired range
•	Polyelectrolyte: the flocculant. Its long polymer chains attach to several particles at once, bridging small flocs into larger, stronger ones
Coagulation vs flocculation: coagulation destabilizes tiny particles so they can come together, while flocculation joins them into larger aggregates that can be separated.
Dissolved Air Flotation (DAF)
Air is dissolved in water under pressure. When the pressure is released, it forms very fine bubbles that attach to the flocs. The bubble-floc combination is lighter than water and rises to the surface as sludge, which is then removed. The clarified water goes on to biological treatment.
Note: the biological treatment stage was not covered during my training, so it is not described in detail.
Primary and secondary treatment reduce the contaminant load, so the biological stage has less material to handle.
6. Conclusion
The training gave me practical exposure to the complete chain of an industrial food plant: water is treated, products are made and packaged, and wastewater is treated before release. I gained technical skills, an understanding of quality and hygiene control, awareness of environmental responsibility, and the confidence to apply classroom chemistry in a real industrial setting.
